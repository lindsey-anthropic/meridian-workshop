We recognise the situation Meridian is in, and we have relevant experience to bring to it.

In the past three years we have worked with several mid-market operations teams who inherited partially-delivered web applications — systems that were functional in places but left with known defects, incomplete migrations, and no test coverage. In each case our work focused on stabilising what was there, closing the gaps, and leaving the in-house IT team in a position to own ongoing changes confidently.

Two engagements are worth highlighting:

**Halcyon Industrial (2024).** Halcyon operates a multi-site distribution network across North America. We took over a Vue/Python inventory system with broken filter logic in the reporting module and no automated tests — a setup that closely mirrors what Meridian describes in §2. We audited the defects, resolved them, and delivered end-to-end test coverage for all critical user flows. Their IT team was able to approve production deployments within two weeks of handoff.

**Thornfield Logistics (2023).** Thornfield's Tokyo-based team was working entirely in English despite a localisation framework that had been partially scaffolded and never completed. We extended i18n coverage to all active views and built a restocking recommendation feature driven by demand forecasts and a configurable budget ceiling — work that maps directly to Meridian's D2 and R2 requirements.

**Our approach for this engagement:**

From there we will follow Meridian's stated priority order — R1 through R4 — with automated tests introduced early so that IT can review and approve changes incrementally rather than waiting for a single final delivery.

We are confident this is work we can do well. We would welcome the chance to discuss it further.
